Question 1. An event that pays out insurance benefits for a small, predictable loss will drive up the cost for such insurance protection in relation to the potential loss. Which requirement of insurable risks addresses the need to avoid insuring small, high-frequency losses?
A. The loss must be definite.
B. The loss must be significant in financial terms.
C. The loss must not be catastrophic to the insurer.
D. The loss must occur by chance.
Correct Answer: B
Rationale: The loss must be significant in financial terms (Ch 1, 3.4). Insuring trivial, expected losses (like routine checkups) would lead to excessive administrative costs relative to the benefit, violating the cost-effectiveness principle of insurable risk.
A is incorrect; "definite" means the time and amount of loss must be known.
C is incorrect; this refers to avoiding risks that could bankrupt the insurer (e.g., war).
D is incorrect; this ensures the loss is random and unexpected.

Question 2. Which form of risk management is best exemplified when a financial planning customer purchases an annuity to ensure income continues after retirement, thus guaranteeing funds regardless of how long they live?
A. Risk Avoidance.
B. Risk Reduction/Control.
C. Risk Transfer.
D. Risk Retention.
Correct Answer: C
Rationale: The purchase of an annuity transfers the risk of longevity (outliving one's savings) from the individual to the insurance company (the risk bearer) in exchange for a premium (Ch 1, 5.4).
A is incorrect; this means eliminating the activity (e.g., never retiring).
B is incorrect; this means minimizing the severity or frequency of the loss (e.g., getting yearly medical checkups).
D is incorrect; this means self-insuring the risk (e.g., relying only on personal savings).

Question 3. When an individual applies for a life insurance sum assured that is excessively large, far exceeding what others in the same financial situation would typically buy, there is a heightened presence of which type of hazard?
A. Health hazard.
B. Physical hazard.
C. Moral hazard.
D. Financial hazard.
Correct Answer: C
Rationale: Moral hazard arises when the presence of insurance encourages less careful behavior or when the applicant knowingly attempts to gain from the policy (Ch 1, 6.3). An unusually large sum assured suggests a non-standard motive, possibly indicating intentional loss.
A and B are incorrect; these relate to existing physical conditions (e.g., bad heart, risky job) that affect longevity, not behavioral or financial motives.
D is incorrect; financial hazard is not a standard term used to categorize risk in this context.

Question 4. The primary objective of the Human Life Value (HLV) approach in calculating insurance needs is to:
A. Determine the client's future earning capacity and use a percentage of that value as the sum assured.
B. Determine the capital required to replace the estimated future lost income of the deceased breadwinner.
C. Calculate the financial capital needed to cover the immediate expenses and outstanding debts only.
D. Estimate the cost of all future non-financial contributions (e.g., childcare, household management) of the deceased.
Correct Answer: B
Rationale: The HLV approach aims to calculate the economic value of an individual's future earnings, discounted to present value, which is then used as the basis for the death benefit sum assured (Ch 2, 2.3).
A is incorrect; HLV discounts the gross earning capacity to find present value.
C is incorrect; this describes the Capital Needs Analysis (CNA) approach.
D is incorrect; HLV focuses on economic/monetary value, not non-financial contributions.

Question 5. What is the core difference between a speculative risk and a pure risk?
A. Speculative risk is always insurable, while pure risk is never insurable.
B. Speculative risk involves only the possibility of loss, whereas pure risk involves the possibility of loss or gain.
C. Pure risk involves no possibility of gain, whereas speculative risk involves the possibility of loss, gain, or no change.
D. Pure risk is only relevant to property insurance, and speculative risk is only relevant to life insurance.
Correct Answer: C
Rationale: Pure risk involves two outcomes: loss or no loss (no possibility of gain), making it the only type of insurable risk. Speculative risk involves three outcomes: loss, gain, or no change (Ch 1, 2.2).

Question 12. A policyholder buys a $500,000 Whole Life policy. This policy satisfies the principle of Indemnity in life insurance by ensuring that the beneficiary:

Question 16. An individual, aged 30, needs life coverage until retirement (age 65). Which of the following policy types offers the lowest premium for this duration, assuming the sum assured remains level throughout?
A. Whole Life Policy.
B. 35-Year Term Policy.
C. Endowment Policy (maturing at age 65).
D. Renewable Term Policy (10-year term, renewable).
Correct Answer: B
Rationale: A straight Term Policy (Option B) covering the exact required duration (35 years) provides pure protection with no savings/cash value component and locks in a level premium for the entire term, making it the least expensive option for this duration. Whole Life (A) and Endowment (C) are significantly more expensive due to cash value accumulation, while Renewable Term (D) gets progressively more expensive upon each renewal.

Question 17. A policyholder owns a Participating Whole Life Policy. The policy accumulates cash value, which is guaranteed, plus non-guaranteed bonuses. The dividends and bonuses declared by the insurer primarily depend on the actual performance of the insurer's:
A. Ordinary Shareholder Fund.
B. Guaranteed Fund (Statutory Deposit Fund).
C. Non-Participating Fund.
D. Participating Fund.
Correct Answer: D
Rationale: Participating policies share in the profits of the insurer's Participating Fund (or Par Fund) (Ch 3, 5.1). The dividends or bonuses are the policyholder's share of the divisible surplus realized from this fund's investment performance, mortality experience, and expense control.

Question 18. Which statement correctly describes the limited payment feature often found in Whole Life Insurance policies?
A. Premiums are paid only up to a fixed age (e.g., age 65), but the death benefit remains in force for life.

C. The cash value accumulation is accelerated to match the policy's face amount by a specified maturity date.
D. The policy automatically converts to a paid-up policy after five consecutive years of full premium payment.
Correct Answer: A
Rationale: A limited payment policy is one where premiums are paid only for a fixed duration or up to a specific age, after which no further premiums are required, but the coverage (death benefit) continues for the whole of life (Ch 3, 4.4).

Question 19. What is the fundamental difference between a Convertible Term policy and a Renewable Term policy?
A. A Convertible Term policy typically has a shorter maximum term than a Renewable Term policy.
B. A Renewable Term policy can be converted to a permanent policy, while a Convertible Term policy cannot be renewed.

D. A Renewable Term policy allows renewal at the same premium rate, while a Convertible Term policy increases its premium upon conversion.
Correct Answer: C
Rationale: A Convertible Term allows the policy to be converted to a permanent plan without proof of insurability (Ch 4, 3.4). A Renewable Term allows the policy to be renewed for another term without proof of insurability (Ch 4, 3.2), but not converted to permanent status. Both features allow the continuation of coverage without new medical exams.

Question 20. Under a Whole Life Policy, the non-forfeiture provision ensures that a policyholder who surrenders their policy can receive:
A. All premiums paid back, without interest.

Question 47. The Level Premium system addresses the practical problem in life insurance where the risk of mortality increases dramatically with age by:
